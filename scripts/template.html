Score legend

What each star rating means in points. Health inspection scores are deficiency points (lower is better); staffing and quality are earned points (higher is better). The overall rating has no point range of its own.

  • Points in parentheses apply when the tag is substandard quality of care. Past-noncompliance immediate jeopardy tags score as a G (20).
  • The health inspection score adds up the last three standard surveys (weighted 1/2, 1/3, 1/6 by recency) plus 36 months of complaint and infection-control surveys, with repeat-revisit points.

How the overall rating is set

  • Start with the health inspection stars.
  • Add one star if staffing is 5★; subtract one if staffing is 1★.
  • Then add one star if quality is 5★; subtract one if quality is 1★. The result stays between 1 and 5.
  • A 1★ health inspection rating can be raised by at most one star. Special Focus Facilities are not star-rated.
  • CMS holds a facility's health inspection stars until its score changes, so a score can sit outside this month's band for its stars. Health inspection stars are curved within each state, so California homes are rated against California.
